Crop Disease

Common Crop Pests and How to Prevent Them

KV Kisan Vision Team 12 September 2026 6 min read

Crop pests can damage leaves, stems, roots, flowers and fruit, reducing both crop quality and yield. The earlier a pest problem is noticed, the more time a farmer has to understand what is happening and choose an appropriate response.

1. Know the common signs of pest activity

Look for holes in leaves, chewed edges, curled leaves, sticky residue, unusual marks, damaged shoots or insects around young growth. Sudden changes in a small area of the field can be an important early warning.

2. Inspect your field regularly

Walk through different parts of the field and inspect both healthy-looking and weaker plants. Check the underside of leaves and areas around stems because some pests can be difficult to see from a distance.

3. Keep the field clean and well managed

Keep weeds under control and remove severely damaged plant material when appropriate. Good field hygiene can reduce places where pests remain between crop cycles.

Spotting a pest early gives you more choices and more time to protect the crop.

4. Use healthy planting material

Start with healthy seeds, seedlings or other planting material from reliable sources. Inspect young plants before bringing them into the main field.

5. Encourage natural pest control

Not every insect in a field is harmful. Some insects can help keep pest populations under control. Where suitable, maintaining a healthy farm ecosystem can support natural pest management.

6. Avoid unnecessary pesticide use

First identify the problem as accurately as possible. If a pesticide is needed, follow the product label and local agricultural guidance, including correct crop, dose, timing and safety precautions.

Common pest warning signs

  • Holes or chewed leaves: inspect for leaf-feeding pests.
  • Curled leaves: check closely for small insects underneath.
  • Sticky leaves: inspect for insects that produce honeydew.
  • Wilting plants: check roots, stems and soil moisture before deciding the cause.
  • Damaged fruits or shoots: inspect feeding marks and affected areas.
  • Sudden patch damage: compare affected plants with healthy plants nearby.

What to do when you notice possible pests

  1. Observe: photograph the affected plant and note where the damage appears.
  2. Inspect: check leaves, stems, roots and nearby plants.
  3. Compare: look at healthy plants to understand what changed.
  4. Identify: use reliable agricultural information or expert guidance.
  5. Act carefully: choose an appropriate management method and follow safety instructions.
  6. Recheck: inspect the field again after taking action.

Technology can help farmers identify problems earlier

A clear photograph of a damaged leaf or plant can provide useful information when investigating a possible pest problem. Digital tools and AI-based crop analysis can provide additional guidance alongside field observation and professional agricultural advice when needed.

The goal is to understand which pests are causing meaningful damage, respond at the right time and protect the crop with practical, responsible management.
