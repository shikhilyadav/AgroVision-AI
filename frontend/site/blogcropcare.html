Crop Care

How to Keep Your Soil Healthy

KV Kisan Vision Team 13 September 2026 6 min read

Healthy soil is the foundation of a healthy crop. Soil provides plants with water, nutrients, air and physical support, so its condition can directly affect crop growth and yield. Simple soil-care practices can help farmers protect this important resource season after season.

1. Know the condition of your soil

Start by observing the soil in different parts of the field. Look for changes in colour, texture, moisture and drainage. Soil that becomes unusually hard, stays wet for too long or dries very quickly may need closer attention.

2. Add organic matter

Organic matter can improve soil structure and help the soil hold moisture and nutrients. Farmyard manure, well-decomposed compost and suitable crop residues can be useful sources when properly prepared and applied according to local recommendations.

3. Avoid unnecessary soil disturbance

Repeated or excessive tillage can disturb soil structure and leave the surface more exposed. Use field practices that are suitable for the crop, soil type and local conditions, while avoiding unnecessary passes over the field.

Healthy soil is not built in one season — small, consistent improvements can make a big difference over time.

4. Protect the soil surface

Mulching and suitable crop cover can help protect the soil from direct rain and wind impact. Keeping soil covered where practical may also help reduce moisture loss and erosion, especially during exposed periods.

5. Manage water carefully

Both too little and too much water can create problems. Poor drainage can leave roots in saturated soil, while insufficient moisture can stress plants. Irrigation should be adjusted according to the crop, soil, weather and field conditions.

Simple ways to improve soil health

  • Observe regularly: check soil texture, moisture and drainage across the field.
  • Use organic matter: apply suitable compost, manure or crop residues when properly prepared.
  • Protect the surface: use suitable mulch or crop cover where practical.
  • Reduce erosion: choose field practices that help keep valuable topsoil in place.
  • Avoid overwatering: maintain moisture without keeping the soil continuously saturated.
  • Keep records: note soil changes, crop performance and the practices that work best.

What farmers should do before the next crop

  1. Inspect: walk through the field and identify hard, compacted, eroded or waterlogged areas.
  2. Check: observe soil moisture, structure and drainage near the crop root zone.
  3. Plan: decide which soil-care practices are suitable for the next crop and field.
  4. Apply: use organic matter, mulch or other suitable practices at the right time.
  5. Record: keep a simple field log so soil-care decisions become easier each season.

Technology can support better soil care

Modern farming tools can combine soil observations, weather information, crop data and field records to support better decisions. Digital tools can help farmers organise this information and identify areas that need attention.

The goal is simple: protect the soil, support healthier crops and maintain productive fields for the future. Start with regular observation and practical soil-care steps, then use technology as an additional layer of information when it can help.
