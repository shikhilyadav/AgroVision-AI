Smart Irrigation: Give Your Crops the Right Amount of Water

KV Kisan Vision Team 12 September 2026 6 min read

Water is one of the most important resources on a farm, but more water does not always mean healthier crops. Too little water can slow growth and reduce yield, while too much can waste water, damage roots and create conditions that favour disease. Smart irrigation is about finding the right balance and applying water when the crop actually needs it.

1. Understand when your crop needs water

Different crops need different amounts of water at different stages of growth. Seedlings, flowering plants and fruiting crops may all have different requirements. Start by observing the crop, soil and weather instead of following the same watering routine every day.

2. Check the soil before irrigating

Soil moisture is one of the simplest signals farmers can use. Check the soil near the root zone rather than judging only by the surface. If the soil is still holding enough moisture, delaying irrigation may save water without stressing the crop.

3. Water the root zone, not the whole field

Efficient irrigation aims to put water where the crop can use it. Drip systems, carefully placed pipes and other targeted methods can reduce unnecessary wetting between crop rows. Keeping leaves dry when possible can also reduce conditions that support some fungal diseases.

Smart irrigation is not about using more water — it is about giving the crop what it needs, when it needs it.

4. Irrigate at the right time of day

In many farming situations, irrigation during cooler parts of the day can reduce losses from evaporation. Morning watering can also give foliage time to dry during the day when overhead irrigation is used. The best timing still depends on crop, soil, weather and irrigation method.

5. Avoid both drought stress and waterlogging

Plants under repeated water stress may wilt, grow slowly or produce smaller yields. On the other hand, constantly saturated soil can reduce oxygen around roots and increase the risk of root problems. Good irrigation keeps the root zone supplied without leaving it continuously soaked.

Simple ways to improve irrigation

  • Observe soil moisture: check the root zone before switching irrigation on.
  • Use targeted watering: deliver water close to the crop where practical.
  • Watch the weather: avoid unnecessary irrigation when useful rainfall is expected.
  • Reduce evaporation: use suitable mulching and irrigation timing where appropriate.
  • Check leaks: inspect pipes, emitters and connections regularly.
  • Track results: note watering dates, crop stage and how the plants respond.

What farmers should do before the next irrigation

  1. Inspect: walk through the field and look for wilting, dry patches or overly wet areas.
  2. Check: feel or measure soil moisture near the crop root zone.
  3. Consider weather: factor in recent rain and the upcoming forecast.
  4. Choose the method: use the most suitable irrigation approach for the crop and field.
  5. Record: keep a simple irrigation log so future decisions become easier.

Technology can make irrigation smarter

Modern farming tools can combine field observations, weather information, soil moisture readings and crop data to support better irrigation decisions. AI can also help organise these signals and turn them into simpler insights, allowing farmers to focus on what needs attention in the field.

The goal is simple: healthier crops, less unnecessary water use and better decisions throughout the season. Start by understanding your soil and crop, then use technology as an extra layer of information when it can help.
