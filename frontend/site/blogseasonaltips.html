Seasonal Farming Tips for Better Yield

KV Kisan Vision Team 12 September 2026 6 min read

Every farming season brings different challenges. Weather, soil condition, water availability, pests and crop growth stages can all affect the final harvest. A simple seasonal plan helps farmers prepare early, protect their crops and make better decisions as the season changes.

1. Prepare the field before planting

Good crop management starts before the first seed goes into the soil. Remove unwanted plant material where necessary, prepare the soil properly and make sure drainage is suitable for the crop. A well-prepared field gives young plants a better environment for early growth.

2. Choose crops according to the season

Different crops perform better under different temperature, rainfall and growing conditions. Before planting, consider the season, local weather pattern, soil type and the amount of water available. Choosing a suitable crop and variety can reduce avoidable stress later in the season.

3. Plan irrigation carefully

Water should be supplied according to the crop's growth stage and soil moisture rather than simply following a fixed routine. Check the root zone before irrigation and avoid both prolonged dryness and unnecessary waterlogging. Efficient watering can help maintain crop health while reducing water waste.

A better harvest often begins with better preparation before the season gets busy.

4. Watch crops regularly

Walk through the field regularly and look for changes in leaf colour, spots, wilting, unusual growth or insect activity. Early observation gives farmers more time to understand a problem and take suitable action before it spreads across a larger area.

5. Protect crops during changing weather

Sudden rain, heat, strong winds and changes in humidity can affect crop health. Keep an eye on weather conditions and adjust irrigation, field visits and other crop-care activities when needed. Good drainage is especially important when heavy rainfall is expected.

6. Keep weeds under control

Weeds compete with crops for water, nutrients, sunlight and space. Regular field inspection makes it easier to identify unwanted growth early. Use suitable weed-management practices for the crop and avoid disturbing healthy plants unnecessarily.

7. Monitor for pests and diseases

Seasonal changes can influence pest and disease pressure. Check leaves, stems, flowers and fruit for unusual marks or damage. If something looks different from normal, take a clear photograph and investigate the possible cause before deciding on a treatment.

Simple seasonal checklist

  • Before planting: prepare soil, check drainage and plan the crop.
  • Early growth: check germination, weeds, moisture and plant health.
  • Mid-season: monitor irrigation, pests, diseases and nutrient needs.
  • Before harvest: inspect crop maturity and prepare harvesting arrangements.
  • After harvest: record what worked well and what should change next season.

What to do when the season changes

  1. Observe: check the crop and field condition before changing your routine.
  2. Assess: consider soil moisture, weather and crop growth stage.
  3. Adjust: modify irrigation and field-care activities where necessary.
  4. Inspect: look for early signs of pests, diseases or plant stress.
  5. Record: keep simple notes to improve decisions for the next season.

Use technology as an extra pair of eyes

Digital farming tools can make seasonal monitoring easier. Weather information, field records, crop photographs and AI-based analysis can provide additional information when farmers are deciding what needs attention. Technology should support field observation, not replace it.

The best seasonal routine is one that is practical enough to follow consistently. Prepare early, observe your crops regularly, manage water carefully and respond to changes before small issues become larger problems. With better planning, every season can become a little more informed.
